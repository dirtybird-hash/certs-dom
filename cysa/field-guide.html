Patterns that repeat across the bank

Trap patterns

"Need" vs. "how to implement"

The need for a change, a finding, or a recommendation is recorded in the lessons learned report. The procedure for making the change, or an ongoing routine like patching, belongs to change control.

Gap between two named metrics

When the question names two metrics and asks about the time between them, answer with the metric that covers that stretch. Detect → act is MTTA. Don't get stuck on whether it says "process" or "metric".

Formal beats technical on "preserve"

"Preserve evidence before notifying the person" calls for a legal hold. Imaging a single device is the technical step that comes with it. When the wording is about law or process, choose the formal mechanism.

Keep critical services up

If a question stresses that downtime is costly, eliminate every answer that takes the service offline (shut down, relocate, re-IP). Contain at the edge with an ACL, isolation, or a WAF instead.

Ransomware: isolate, don't power off

Pulling the network keeps RAM evidence intact. Powering off destroys it. "Contain and preserve" means isolate.

Who talks to whom

Media and customers go to PR. Police and regulators go to Legal. Employee misconduct or employee data goes to HR. Cost of the incident goes to senior management. Analysts never comment, even off the record.

Customer PII ≠ HR

Distractors list customer data next to employee data. HR handles only cases where employees are the actor or the victims.

Event vs. incident

An event is anything you can observe. An incident threatens confidentiality, integrity, or availability, or breaks policy. An unexplained reboot is an event until evidence ties it to malicious activity.

Infrastructure is not capability

In the Diamond Model, C2 servers, domains, and IPs are infrastructure. Malware, exploits, and TTPs are capability. Motive belongs with the adversary, and the people targeted are the victim.

Plug-in update, sudden new finding

If one finding appears on every host right after a scanner update, suspect a false positive, and verify it before you accept, exempt, or fix it.

Unknown threats need behavior

Signatures, anti-malware, and daily IoC updates catch only known threats. When the question says unknown, zero-day, or new variant, choose behavior, anomaly, or heuristic detection.

Honeypot / sandbox / sinkhole

To study attacker TTPs, use a honeypot. To safely run a suspicious file, use a sandbox. To redirect bot or C2 traffic and see who's infected, use a sinkhole. To administer high-value servers, use a jump box.

"Only…" in planning answers

For the communication plan and stakeholder lists, the answer that includes everyone wins. Details are still shared only with trusted, need-to-know parties during the investigation.

Watch NOT and EXCEPT

About a dozen items in the bank ask which choice is not a factor, control, or source. Underline the negation before you read the options.

Access control and network placement

  • Role-based: access by job function. Rule-based: a system-wide rule, such as time of day. Location-based: where the connection comes from. Context-based: several signals combined.
  • Federation uses the same identity across organizations. SSO is one sign-in across apps within one organization.
  • VLAN: a quick logical group for a small team. Air gap: no network connection at all. Jump box: the single hardened path to administer servers. DMZ + NGFW: a public server that also needs IPS.
  • SSL tunnel VPN carries web and non-web traffic. A portal VPN covers only browser-based applications.
  • Cleartext SMTP seen in a capture means transport encryption is missing. Sniffing risk in general calls for IPsec.
  • Ping sweeps from outside: block inbound ICMP echo at the edge.
  • Firmware updates: digital signatures prove both authenticity and integrity. A hash alone proves only integrity.
Syslog severity

0 Emergency · 1 Alert · 2 Critical · 3 Error · 4 Warning · 5 Notice · 6 Informational · 7 Debug. Lower numbers are more severe, so "log warnings and worse" means level 4 and lower.

Forensic basics the bank keeps coming back to

  • Hash the source before imaging, hash the copy after, and compare. That's how you prove integrity.
  • dd makes a bit-for-bit image, including slack and unallocated space. cp, mv, and tar copy only files.
  • Data carving finds files by their header signature. It assumes the file isn't fragmented and that its starting signature still exists.
  • Collect the most volatile evidence first, and RAM comes before disk.

1.4 Threat intelligence & hunting

30 bank items · drill 65%
Hypothesis→Collect data→Analyze TTPs→Find & remove hidden threats→Feed detections
  • A hunt starts with a hypothesis. It's usually triggered by a suspected IoC and aims to find attackers who are already hiding inside.
  • TTPs tell you how capable and dangerous an actor is. MITRE ATT&CK is the free catalog of them. STIX is the format for threat data, and TAXII is the protocol for sharing it, for example across an ISAC or a consortium.
  • OSINT is public information only: search engines, social media, the company website. An internal file server is not OSINT.
  • APT: stays hidden for a long time, and its goal is to watch or steal data.
  • A signature-based NIDS misses new threats. Machine learning detection depends on good, relevant training data.
  • Getting traffic to a monitor: a network tap or a port mirror (SPAN).
  • Trusted Foundry (US DoD) means suppliers can show a chain of custody for chips. That's the answer to "prove component authenticity for a government bid."

1.5 Process improvement

5 bank items · drill 88%
  • Too many false positives reaching Tier 2: give Tier 1 triage training and tune the alerts.
  • Users get local admin just to install software: build golden images per role instead.
  • Protect API keys by restricting them to known IPs and requiring TLS.
  • YANG models with automation: RESTCONF + Python. DevSecOps: build security into every stage automatically.

2.3 Prioritizing vulnerabilities

13 bank items · drill 100% (11)
  • Severity × exposure × asset value. A 9.1 on three internet-facing servers and a 7.7 on every workstation outrank a 9.8 on one isolated, non-critical box.
  • The higher the asset's value, the higher the remediation priority.
  • CVSS base metrics: Attack Vector tells you whether it's reachable over the network. Confidentiality Impact tells you whether data can be read or stolen. Requirement metrics are environmental metrics, not base metrics.
  • Threat types: known (DDoS patterns, cleartext transmission), unknown, and zero-day (no fix exists yet). Anti-malware does nothing against unknown threats.
  • A virus got through an IoC-only IDS: add behavior-based detection. If an exhibit shows missing OS patches, the recommendation is patch management.

Requirements (security needs)→Test planning→Design→Build→Test→Deploy→Maintain
TestingAnswers
StressCan it handle the new transaction or throughput targets?

Static analysisData flow analysis, Hoare logic, symbolic execution. Fuzzing is dynamic.
  • Continuous integration merges validated changes into the main branch quickly. Continuous delivery keeps code always releasable. Continuous deployment automates all the way to production, the most automated of the three.
  • Allowlisting (whitelisting) is how you stop unapproved software. Blocklists are never complete.
  • BYOD minimum posture: NAC. Biggest SaaS risk: password/credential management. Self-driving vehicles: the CAN bus.
  • To reduce attack surface, close unused ports, remove unused services, segment the network, and patch. Threat modeling: attack vector analysis lists the paths in, and total attack surface covers growth such as IoT. IoT problems: a wider attack surface and default passwords.
Remediation inhibitors (filed under 4.1 in the bank)

An SLA (uptime promises) and an MOU (agreed terms with a partner) can both delay patching. EULAs, AUPs, and NDAs don't. Prioritize remediation by criticality and difficulty of implementation, and test patches in a sandbox or isolated segment before rolling them out.

Domain 3 · Incident Response

3.1 Attack methodology frameworks

9 bank items · drill 88%
1 Recon→2 Weaponization→3 Delivery→4 Exploitation→5 Installation→6 C2→7 Actions
  • Random-looking subdomains in DNS TXT queries mean C2 or tunneling over DNS (stage 6).
  • Diamond Model: Adversary (who and why) · Capability (malware and TTPs) · Infrastructure (C2 servers, domains, IPs) · Victim (people and systems targeted).
  • OSSTMM covers security testing in general. The OWASP Testing Guide covers only web app pen testing. ATT&CK is the TTP knowledge base.
  • A broadcast ping such as ping -b to the .255 address is topology discovery.

3.2 Incident response activities

17 bank items · drill 93%
  • Once an incident is confirmed, the next step is containment. Isolation stops traffic so the attacker can't move laterally.
  • Legal hold: identify relevant data and preserve it as it is. It covers email and file servers, and nothing can be deleted or changed.
  • "What happened before the incident" means logs (log viewer or SIEM). Firewall logs record IPs, ports, rules, and actions, but not MAC addresses.
  • Re-imaging returns a system to a clean, known state. Many authenticator prompts in a row before an accept is MFA fatigue.
  • Hashes don't match and the file is hiding inside another file: exfiltration plus steganography.
  • Unexplained restarts on VMs behind physical security point to malware in the VMs, not the hosts.
  • Impact categories include downtime, recovery time, and economic cost. Rule out data integrity when nothing was altered.

3.3 Preparation & post-incident activity

Metrics timeline

Compromise— MTTD →Alert— MTTA →Response— MTTC →Contained— MTTR (remediate) →Normal business
  • Faster response comes from a 24/7 SOC and SOAR. Fresh signatures improve detection, and tested backups improve recovery.
  • Improving mean time to remediate means returning to normal business sooner. The IR plan is the factor with the biggest effect on it.
  • RCA finds the underlying cause so the incident doesn't happen again. Lessons learned are delivered in the Post-Incident Activity phase.
